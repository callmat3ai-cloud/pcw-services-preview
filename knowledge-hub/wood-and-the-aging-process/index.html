Wood and the Aging Process

How moisture, sun, and time break wood down — and when repair becomes replacement.

Read the guide ↓

In this guide

  1. Record how wood is changing
  2. Ask about repair versus replacement
  3. Plan for concealed information
  4. Planning checklist
Browse all guides

Record how wood is changing

Wood components are exposed to moisture, sunlight, and time. Paint failure, staining, splitting, or visible deterioration can prompt questions about condition, but the appearance alone does not establish how far damage extends. Record the component and its location rather than labeling every change as dry rot.

Ask about repair versus replacement

Share available repair history and photographs with the contractor. Ask what can be assessed from the accessible surface and whether further review is needed. The appropriate repair depends on the condition and role of the component; a general guide cannot decide whether a particular member can be retained.

Plan for concealed information

Where work may expose hidden damage, ask how findings will be photographed, described, and brought back for approval. Keep the original scope distinct from additional work. After completion, retain the repair record and any property-specific maintenance guidance for the next review.

Planning checklist

  • Identify location and exposure.
  • Describe visible changes without diagnosing.
  • Ask what remains concealed or unverified.
  • Document repair decisions and follow-up care.

General planning information, not a property assessment, installation instruction, legal opinion, or determination of insurance coverage. Ask the appropriate qualified professional about your property's specific conditions.
